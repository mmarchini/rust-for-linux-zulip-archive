Philipp Stanner (Oct 01 2026 at 12:28):

Why is it that we do not revoke a Revocable when it drops? @Danilo Krummrich
That seems strange; The only reason why it exists is to be revoked at some point

Alice Ryhl (Oct 01 2026 at 12:38):

If it's dropped, nobody else can touch it, so revoking it is a no-op.

Philipp Stanner (Oct 01 2026 at 12:41):

Well, but it still makes no sense to do it, or does it?

I suppose we don't print a warning about it because it can be hypothetically a legitimate use-case

Alice Ryhl (Oct 01 2026 at 12:42):

I definitely think that there are use-cases for revocable where it might get dropped without having been revoked first, if that's what you mean.

Boqun Feng (Oct 01 2026 at 12:49):

You only need to revoke() if there is (or will be) a reader. Dropping requires you to unshare Revocable first, so you don't need to worry about a concurrent reader or a future reader.

Boqun Feng (Oct 01 2026 at 12:52):

And I don't think we need to defend the existence of a no-op? I mean it's a no-op that is correct.

Boqun Feng (Oct 01 2026 at 12:57):

For "legitimate" use case, you can think of a process of initializing some driver data but it fails in the middle after creating some Revocables, since these Revocables have never been shared, cleanup for them should not be involved with revoke() (i.e. no synchronize_rcu()).
